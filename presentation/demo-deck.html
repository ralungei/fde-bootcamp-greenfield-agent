What I type
1I'm cooking a special dinner on Saturday for someone I really want to impress. I know nothing about wine. I'm happy to spend a bit more than usual, I want a proper bottle. Where do I start?
2We're having ribeye steak. Honestly I don't know what I like. I've only ever bought whatever was on offer.
3You just said full-bodied: what does that actually mean? And is that a good thing with steak?
4Between those two, which one would you open if it was your dinner? And why?
5Perfect, add it to my trolley.
6Now I need something to nibble on before we sit down, something that goes with that wine. I want the whole evening to feel special.
7Add the cheese as well.
What I say

Open: “I'm not a persona we invented: this is literally me. I know nothing about wine. I have a dinner that matters and I don't want to get it wrong. Watch it take me from ‘no idea’ to a bottle I'm proud of, and then build the rest of the evening around it.”

After 1: “No grape, no country, no price filter. I gave it the situation, and the situation is what the customer actually has in their head.”

Before 3: “In a normal shop this is where I give up. I nod, I pretend I understood, and I buy the one with the nice label.”

After 3: “A search bar makes you already know the answer. This one teaches me while I shop, and a customer who understands what they're buying trades up.”

After 6: “It's not upselling me. It knows which bottle is in my trolley and builds the evening around it.”

What I type
1Forget the dinner for a second. What did I order last time?
2Plan three dinners for me this week. I'm terrible at organising and I always end up throwing food away, so make the recipes share ingredients, so nothing gets wasted. Nothing too complicated.
3Perfect. Add everything I need for all three to my trolley. And add my usual basics on top: water, milk, coffee.
·Then open any product and press “Ask Smart Shopper about this product”.
What I say

Before 2: “My real problem: I buy fresh food with good intentions, use half of it, and the rest rots, because by the time I work out what else to cook with it, it's gone. Every week.”

While it works: “It's not looking up three recipes. It's solving an optimisation problem: three meals, overlapping ingredients, minimum waste, then mapping every ingredient onto a real product in the right pack size.”

After 3, say nothing. Let the trolley fill. Count to three. Then: “That's my week. Under two minutes, from an empty basket.”

On the dial: “Today it leans premium because I asked to impress. That's a dial you own. A lever for you, not just a nicer experience for me.”
